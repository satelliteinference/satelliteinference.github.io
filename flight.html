FIRST FLIGHT / A DAY ON THE HOST

One day of
the first flight.

Our first flight is a ready-made, space-rated NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin flight computer, carried as a hosted payload on a partner spacecraft. The host switches it on for sessions, caps its power, cools it through the plate it is bolted to and carries a small daily data allowance for it. This simulator runs that day: how many tasks finish, what comes down, how warm the computer gets and what radiation to expect. The scenarios show what our runtime has to do inside those limits.

4 sessions of 15 minutes at 30 W: 18 tasks a day on 28.5 Wh, and 0.98 MB of the 5.0 MB daily allowance. Compute time sets the limit, not the link.

01 / WHAT THE HOST GIVES

A slot, not a satellite.

SESSIONS

Powered 60 minutes a day.

A hosted payload runs when its host can spare the power. With 4 sessions of 15 minutes the computer is on for 4.2% of the day. Every session starts with a boot and a model load, 90 seconds here, which leaves 54 minutes of compute.

POWER

A cap, held by software.

NVIDIA's Jetson AGX Orin Industrial module runs between 15 W and 75 W. The host sets the cap, 30 W here, and the runtime keeps the computer under it: 28.5 Wh a day and about 1.5 Wh for each of 18 tasks.

DATA

Answers fit. Images do not.

The daily allowance, 5 MB here, is shared with telemetry. A 50 kB result fits 98 times a day, so every task's answer comes down. An 18 MB package of image chips would take 3.7 days to send. On this flight the answer is computed on board and the images stay there.

02 / WHAT THE RUNTIME HAS TO DO

Finish the job inside the limits.

POWER CUTS

Every session ends in a power-off.

A 20-minute job never fits a 15-minute session. Without checkpoints it starts again at every power-on and no result ever comes down: 54 minutes of compute are discarded each day. With a checkpoint every 30 seconds the same job finishes 2.7 times a day.

HEAT

The host's plate decides.

The computer sheds its heat into the plate it is bolted to. On a plate between 10 °C and 25 °C it peaks at 27.4 °C, 28 K under its 55 °C limit. On a plate that swings up to 56 °C the runtime leaves 1.5 of 4 sessions a day unstarted, and the computer peaks at 54.6 °C while it runs, under its limit.

MORE SERVICE

Power buys tasks. Data buys detail.

At 60 W the same tasks finish in 111 seconds instead of 180, if speed follows power as assumed: 29 tasks a day instead of 18. A second computer doubles the count to 36 and keeps the mission alive if one fails. A larger allowance is what lets image chips come down.

03 / RADIATION

A short day in a mild orbit.

INTERRUPTS

About 0.003 in 6 months.

Published proton tests of an Orin-class module give 2.7 interrupts a year when it is powered all the time at 525 km. Our plan powers it 4.2% of the time and keeps sessions outside the South Atlantic Anomaly, where 96% of the hits fall in 6.6% of the time.

FAULT DATA

It has to be sought.

A flight that avoids the anomaly proves the service and measures almost no faults. Sessions centred on the anomaly's passes, 9 minutes each, bring the expected count to about 1.2 interrupts and 1.7 logged upsets in 6 months. The session plan decides what the flight can measure.

DOSE

264 rad in 6 months.

Dose builds up whether the computer is on or off: 264 rad behind 3 mm of aluminium, 0.7% of the 37 krad at which published tests of the module saw their first failures.

04 / METHOD AND LIMITS

A budget, stepped through a day.

The model steps through 30 days in 5-second steps while the computer is on. Sessions are evenly spaced. The computer is one lumped mass of 4 kg on a conductance of 4 W/K to the host plate, whose temperature swings once per orbit. Tasks run at the power cap; at another cap their speed follows (P / 30 W) to the power 0.7. Results queue for what is left of the daily allowance after telemetry, and a package larger than a day's allowance goes down in pieces. Automated tests check the energy balance, the thermal step response, the checkpoint logic, the data cap and the radiation scaling on every build.

Not modelled: the host's actual schedule and its link to the ground, temperatures inside the computer (the limit applies at its plate side), the power profile during boot, compression and retransmission of results, and faults other than proton-induced ones. Radiation figures are expected values scaled from the model on the radiation page and inherit its limits.

The first flight targets late 2027. The session pattern, the power cap and the data allowance are working assumptions that the hosting agreement replaces. Task time, speed against power and boot time are measured on the engineering unit in the first months of the plan. Vendors and hosts are named once agreements are signed.

Power range: NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin Industrial, 15 W to 75 W (NVIDIA Jetson modules documentation). Radiation rates and dose: the Orin-class module on the radiation page, with its sources.
