01 / WHAT YOU ARE SEEING

Sunlight is a design choice.

THE ORBIT

The line between day and night.

A dawn–dusk orbit is sun-synchronous: Earth's equatorial bulge turns the orbit plane once a year, so it stays aligned with the boundary between day and night.

THE SEASON

A short eclipse season.

Press Year to watch it. Around the June solstice the tilt of Earth's axis turns the orbit plane toward the Sun line. When β, the angle between them, falls below about 65°, the orbit clips Earth's shadow: up to about 20 minutes per orbit, for roughly three months. Solar arrays and batteries are sized for that worst day.

THE ECONOMICS

Fewer kilograms per kilowatt.

More hours of sunlight per kilogram launched means fewer solar panels and batteries for the same compute: a direct input to the cost of an orbital GPU-hour. Size a node to see the arrays, battery and radiators.

02 / METHOD AND LIMITS

Real geometry, screening accuracy.

Circular two-body orbits with J2 nodal precession, a low-precision solar ephemeris for 2027 and a cylindrical Earth shadow without penumbra or atmospheric refraction. The annual results match the Satellite Inference orbit model v3.1 to within half a percentage point; automated tests check this on every build.
